# Privacy policy

Last updated: 10 November 2026

This page says what SACCO Ledger knows about you, why, and what you can do about it. It is written in plain words on purpose.

SACCO Ledger is run by [YOUR COMPANY NAME], [YOUR ADDRESS], Uganda. Data protection contact: [NAME OF DATA PROTECTION OFFICER], [CONTACT EMAIL], [CONTACT PHONE]. Registration with the Personal Data Protection Office: [PDPO NUMBER].

## The short version

- We keep records. We never hold, move or touch your money.
- We collect only what a savings group needs to keep honest books.
- We do not sell your data and we show no adverts.
- You can see your data, download it, fix it, and ask us to close your account.

## What we keep

- **About you:** your name, phone number, email if you gave one, and a photo if you added one.
- **For your group, only if the group asks:** your national ID number and the name and phone of your next of kin.
- **Money records:** payments you reported, the transaction ID, the channel (MTN, Airtel, bank or cash), your loans, repayments, fines, emergency claims and share-out amounts.
- **Group life:** meeting attendance and the role you hold.
- **Sign-ins:** the last 50 times you signed in, with the kind of device, so you can spot a stranger.
- **Your choices:** language, look, text size and country.

Your app PIN and your fingerprint never leave your phone. We cannot see them.

## Why we keep it

- To show you and your group who paid what, and when.
- To let two different people check each payment, so nobody can cheat alone.
- To keep an audit trail that cannot be changed in secret.
- To keep your account safe.

The law lets us do this because you agreed when you joined a group, and because your group needs the records to run.

## Who can see what

- **You** see your own records.
- **Your group's officers** (administrator, treasurer, secretary) see the group's records, because checking them is their job.
- **Other members** see group totals and goals. They do not see your phone, your ID, or your loans.
- **The platform team** can see groups and accounts to give support and to stop abuse. Everything they change is written in a history.
- **Nobody else.** We give data to the authorities only when the law forces us to.

## Where it is stored

Records are stored with Supabase, a cloud database company, on servers outside Uganda. The data is locked while it travels and while it rests. You agree to this when you join a group. Ask us for the country of the server if you want to know.

If your group turns on online payments, the payment itself is handled by Flutterwave or Pesapal under their own privacy rules. We receive only the result: paid or not paid, the amount and the reference. We never see card numbers or mobile money PINs.

If you sign in with Google, Google tells us your name, email and photo. Nothing else.

## How long we keep it

- Money records stay for as long as the group exists and for 10 years after, because groups must be able to prove their books.
- If you close your account, we remove your phone, ID, next of kin, photo, choices and sign-in history. Past payments and loans stay with your name, as proof for the group.
- Sign-in history keeps only your last 50 sign-ins.

## Your rights

Under Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act you can:

- **See your data:** My Account, then Download my data.
- **Fix it:** change it in My Account, or ask your group secretary.
- **Close your account:** My Account, then Close my account. You can cancel until it is done.
- **Say no:** you can leave a group at any time. Records already made stay.
- **Complain:** write to us first. If you are not happy, write to the Personal Data Protection Office, pdpo.go.ug.

## Children

SACCO Ledger is for adults. Do not use it if you are under 18.

## If something goes wrong

If your data is ever seen by someone who should not see it, we will tell you and the Personal Data Protection Office as soon as we know, and say what we are doing about it.

## Changes

If this page changes in a way that matters, the app will tell you before the change starts.
